# Furnishing a Second Home Between Vienna and New York, Ready on Arrival

**In short:** When you furnish a second home, you plan for arriving, not for round-the-clock living. A good pied-à-terre is habitable at once, needs little care, survives weeks without anyone in it and still feels personal. Between Vienna and New York the task has a second layer: two cities and two building traditions, joined by one shared signature. The gain for you: you land, unlock the door and are home.

You step out of the taxi after a night flight, suitcase in hand, and the key turns in a lock that does not feel like yours. Now picture the same moment differently: the heating has warmed the rooms, the bed is made, and there is a kettle and tea in the drawer where your hand looks for them. Think of a woman in her early fifties, whom we shall call Ms K. She has lived and worked in Manhattan for twenty years, and in Vienna she has her family, her school friends and the city she grew up in. Four or five times a year she flies over for two or three weeks, and until now she stayed in a hotel or with her sister. Now she has bought a two-room apartment in the third district: 68 square metres, second floor, Gründerzeit (the grand period of Viennese building in the late nineteenth century). Her wish sounds simple. When she unlocks the door, she should not feel like a guest.

## What makes a second home different

A second home is not a smaller main home. It follows another rhythm. The apartment stands empty for weeks, then it is lived in intensively, often with jet lag, often with visitors. In between, nothing should happen that cannot be watched from a distance.

That changes almost every planning decision. In a main home a plant may stand on the windowsill, a linen curtain may fade a little in the sun, the fridge is always full. In a pied-à-terre the apartment has to work well when nobody is there, and in the first hours after arrival, without shopping and without unpacking.

A pied-à-terre is, literally, a foot on the ground: a small, well-equipped city apartment that you live in regularly but not permanently. In New York the term is firmly established; in Vienna such apartments have been growing in number for years.

> A second home is a success if, after unlocking the door, you put the kettle on before you unpack a single suitcase.

To get there, we do not start with style. We start with you.

## The guiding question stays the same: how do you want to live?

With a second home too, the beginning is daily life. For Ms K. we played through a typical week in Vienna during the initial consultation.

She lands early in the morning, is in the apartment by nine and sleeps until midday. In the afternoon she meets friends, and in the evening she likes to cook for three or four people. Two days a week she works with New York, usually from 3 p.m. to 9 p.m. because of the time difference. At weekends her niece sometimes comes and stays the night.

Very concrete requirements follow:

- **A bedroom that really goes dark**, even at twelve noon.
- **A small but fully fledged kitchen** for dinners with guests.
- **A workspace with good light and a good camera angle** for video calls in the evening.
- **A place for guests to sleep** that does not get in the way day to day.
- **Storage for a second wardrobe**, so the suitcase stays light.

This list is not about style. It is the framework on which everything creative rests later. And the first test of that framework is a single moment: the arrival.

## Arriving without unpacking

The most important moment in the life of a second home is arrival. It decides whether the apartment feels like a home or a holiday flat.

So we plan a deliberate basic kit that always stays:

1. **A complete second wardrobe** for the season of your most frequent stays, plus a compartment for basics that are always needed.
2. **Bed linen and towels in two sets**, so the bed is freshly made on arrival and can be changed before departure.
3. **A stock of long-life staples** in a well-planned kitchen drawer: tea, coffee, oil, salt, pasta, tinned goods.
4. **Chargers and adapters in fixed places**, including sockets with USB right at the bed and at the workspace.
5. **An arrival spot in the hallway**: a shelf for suitcases, a hook for the jacket, a compartment for keys and post.

This kit needs space. In 68 square metres, a good [storage concept](/magazin/stauraumkonzept-ganze-wohnung/) is not a luxury but a precondition. For Ms K., a floor-to-ceiling fitted wardrobe in the hallway takes the second wardrobe, the suitcases and a small household area. Our joinery workshop Tischlerkultur builds such pieces to the millimetre, in the Viennese carpentry tradition that has carried the family since 1935.

Imagine the first morning: you open the hallway wardrobe, your own coat hangs where it should, and the suitcase never leaves the shelf. A kit like this only works if the surfaces survive the weeks of absence in between.

## Materials that can cope with absence

An apartment that stands empty for weeks puts special demands on surfaces and textiles. Sun streams in unhindered, nobody airs the rooms, dust settles. Yet the apartment should feel warm and personal, not like a hotel room.

| Area | What we look for | Typical choice |
|---|---|---|
| Floor | calm, robust, unaffected by dust | oiled oak parquet, natural stone |
| Worktop | easy to care for, heat-resistant, stain-resistant | Cosentino Dekton, dense natural stone |
| Curtains | colourfast, blackout, easily washable | linen blend with blackout lining |
| Upholstery | hard-wearing, removable covers | tightly woven wool or linen |
| Plants | robust, or deliberately none | dried branches, objects instead of greenery |

One subject deserves particular attention: fading. Natural fibres, oiled timber and some kinds of leather change under UV light. In an occupied apartment this happens evenly and unnoticed. In an empty one with the curtains open, shadow edges appear. We therefore recommend darkening the rooms before you leave or planning an automatic control. Which surfaces are truly insensitive in the long run is covered in our article on [easy-care surfaces](/magazin/oberflaechen-pflegeleicht-ehrlich-bewertet/).

What looks after the apartment while you are away?

## Technology that keeps watch from afar

For a second home, a well-thought-out smart home you cannot see makes particular sense. It is not about blinking gadgets, but about a few reliable functions.

- **Remote control of the heating**, so the apartment is warm on arrival and not heated needlessly while you are away.
- **Water sensors under the sink, washing machine and washbasin** that send a message in the event of a leak. With a shut-off valve if required.
- **Blinds or curtains on a timer**, which regulate light and sun protection and suggest an occupied apartment.
- **Stable Wi-Fi** that works without a restart when you return after three months.

It matters that everything can also be operated without an app. Your niece, the property management (Hausverwaltung) or a trusted person who looks in on the apartment must be able to switch on lights and heating without instruction. You can read more about this approach in our article on a [smart home you don't see](/magazin/smart-home-sinnvoll-unsichtbar/).

**What doing nothing costs.** Without a plan, a second home becomes a place you tidy up before you can live in it. Every trip starts with an hour of airing, shopping and searching for the adapter, and the apartment you bought for arriving behaves like a holiday let. The longer that goes on, the less you are there.

Now to the harder question: how can one signature hold two such different cities?

## Two cities, one signature

Anyone with a second home often lives between two places built very differently. A Viennese Gründerzeit house has high rooms, box-type windows, double-leaf doors and herringbone parquet. A New York prewar apartment often has lower ceilings, radiators beneath the windows and an entirely different handling of light. A loft in Tribeca, in turn, has steel columns and open floor space.

The temptation is to furnish both the same way. That rarely works. A Viennese Altbau styled like a Manhattan loft loses its charm, and vice versa.

What we aim for is a shared signature, visible in a few recurring elements:

- **The same timber** for built-ins and furniture, such as an oiled oak that appears in both apartments.
- **A related colour palette**, adapted to the light in each place.
- **A light fitting that stands in both apartments**, for example a floor lamp beside the reading chair. How light fittings are integrated into a plan is explained in our article on [lighting design](/magazin/leuchten-auswaehlen-teil-der-lichtplanung/).
- **The same habits in the layout**: the bed faces the window, the reading lamp is on the left, the coffee machine is in the same place in the kitchen.

These repetitions are subtle, yet they help the body arrive faster after the flight. The method behind it is in our article on the [material concept as a common thread](/magazin/materialkonzept-roter-faden-durch-alle-raeume/).

### What is different in New York

Anyone renovating in New York meets other procedures than in Vienna. In many Manhattan residential buildings every renovation needs the approval of the co-op board or building management, often with separate agreements, proof of insurance and fixed working hours. Electrical appliances and light fittings must be suitable and approved for the American mains supply. That also applies to European-made furniture with integrated lighting. We clarify these points with the responsible parties on site before planning begins.

In Vienna the questions are different: the consent of the co-owners under the Austrian Condominium Act (Wohnungseigentumsgesetz), a building notification or building permit for certain interventions, the rules of the house. An overview is in our article on [permits for Altbau conversions in Vienna](/magazin/genehmigungen-altbau-wien-umbau/).

Two cities, two sets of rules, and you in neither of them at the time. How do you plan all this from far away?

## Planning from a distance

Most people who furnish a second home are not on site while it is planned. Ms K. was in Vienna twice in the six months between purchase and moving in, each time for a week. The rest ran through video calls, photos, sample packages and a shared folder of plans.

For that to work, you need clear structures:

1. **One fixed contact** for all questions, from concept to handover.
2. **Fixed call times** that respect the time difference. Between Vienna and New York it is usually six hours.
3. **Decision documents with images and samples**, which arrive by post and not only on screen.
4. **Regular photo documentation of the building site**, so you see progress without flying.
5. **Concentrated on-site meetings** for the decisions that can only be made in the room: colour samples, lighting mood, furniture placement.

## Small, but complete

A pied-à-terre is usually smaller than a main home. That is an opportunity, not a restriction. Small apartments demand clear decisions, and clear decisions make rooms calm.

For Ms K. the kitchen is only 2.40 metres long yet fully equipped: an induction hob with integrated extractor, a compact oven with steam function, a fridge with its own freezer compartment. It opens onto the living space, because that is where she cooks for guests. How to plan a small kitchen that feels large is shown in our article on the [small kitchen, thought big](/magazin/kleine-kueche-gross-denken/).

The guest bed is a wide daybed in the study with a drawer beneath for linen. By day it is a sofa for reading, by night a full-sized bed for her niece. The desk is positioned so that behind the screen you see a calm wall of books and not a bed.

Picture an evening in October: onions in the pan, friends at the counter, the New York call finished an hour ago and the laptop closed in the study. That is the apartment she is buying with this plan.

> The goal for an apartment like Ms K.'s fits into one sentence: never again to feel like a visitor in Vienna.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a pied-à-terre?

A pied-à-terre is a smaller, well-equipped city apartment that is lived in regularly but not permanently. Stays of a few days up to several weeks are typical. The planning therefore concentrates more than for a main home on arrival, easy care and security during absence, and that is exactly what we plan for.

### What should you consider first when furnishing a second home?

First, daily life during your stays: how long, how often, alone or with visitors, with or without work. Bedroom, kitchen, workspace and storage follow from that. Style and furniture come afterwards and are then far easier to decide.

### Can you have an apartment in Vienna planned and converted from New York?

Yes, with clear structures it works well. What matters is a fixed contact, call times that suit the time difference, samples by post and regular photos from the building site. Some decisions, such as colour samples and lighting mood, are best made during a few well-prepared visits on site. You never have to organise it alone.

### Which smart-home functions make sense for a second home?

Mainly remote control of the heating, water sensors with notifications, timed blinds or curtains and reliable Wi-Fi. Everything should also work without an app, so that visitors or a trusted person can manage. More technology is rarely better.

### Should both apartments be furnished the same way?

Usually not. A Viennese Altbau and a New York apartment have different proportions, different light and a history of their own. Better is a shared signature of recurring materials, colours and habits, while each apartment respects its place.

### May I let my second home in Vienna on a short-term basis?

Strict rules apply to short-term letting in both Vienna and New York, and they have tightened in recent years. Whether and to what extent letting is permitted depends on location, zoning and house rules, and should be checked legally before planning. For the furnishing, the main question is whether you need a lockable private area for personal belongings. Tell us in the first call and we plan accordingly.
