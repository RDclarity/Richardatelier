# Boutique Hotel Interior Design: Rooms Where Guests Feel at Home by the Second Evening

**In short:** A hotel room feels like home when it makes a guest's everyday movements effortless: arriving, unpacking, sleeping well, showering in peace, reading in comfort. Anyone fitting out a boutique hotel or holiday apartment therefore plans workflows, lighting, acoustics and storage first, and then chooses materials that feel homely yet withstand daily operation. Decor alone does not create this feeling. The reward is guests who arrive, exhale and feel looked after.

A guest drops the suitcase on the bed because there is nowhere else, hunts for the switch in the dark, and spends the first evening getting used to a room that does not know them. Now imagine a room in which the suitcase has its place, the light comes in gentle scenes, and the first night is a good night. Let us call them Clara and Martin. In the Josefstadt they have taken over a Gründerzeit building (a late nineteenth-century Viennese apartment block) that used to house a simple guesthouse. Twelve rooms on the lower floors, plus two holiday apartments in the converted attic. The rooms are clean but interchangeable: fitted carpet, a desk nobody uses, a ceiling light in the middle, a television opposite the bed. On the first tour, Clara says a sentence that shapes the whole project: "I'd like someone to feel at home by the second evening." This article shows what it means to design a boutique hotel in which guests feel at home, and what of that applies to holiday apartments.

## What makes a hotel room feel like home

A boutique hotel is a small, individually run house, usually with fewer than fifty rooms, whose character arises from the place, the architecture and the attitude of the hosts. Furnishing such a house differs from classic hotel room design above all in one respect: it is not about a repeatable standard room but about rooms with personality that nevertheless work reliably.

Our guiding question for flats is "How would you like to live?" In a hotel we ask it on behalf of the guest. How does someone want to live who is in an unfamiliar city for three nights? They want to put down their suitcase without it being in the way. They want to know where the light switch is when they wake at two in the morning. They want to stand in front of the mirror in daylight in the morning and read in warm light in the evening.

The feeling of home thus arises from the self-evident. A room that has to be explained is a foreign room. A room you understand intuitively quickly becomes familiar. This self-evidence is the result of precise planning, not of chance.

> Home is where you no longer have to think about where the light switch is.

Leaving rooms interchangeable has a cost you notice in what guests do not say: they were comfortable, and they do not remember. A room that looks good on day one but wears badly costs again, in worn edges, in repairs, in rooms taken out of service. Neither is dramatic, but both repeat every day. So what happens in the first two minutes?

## Arriving: the first two minutes in the room

The first minutes decide how a guest experiences a room. They open the door, look for the light, put down the suitcase, take off their coat. If each of these steps has a place, they relax. If they have to put the suitcase on the bed because there is nowhere else, the stay begins with a small irritation.

For Clara and Martin we planned every room with a short entrance zone, just as in a flat. A hook and an open wardrobe next to the door, a bench beneath for taking off shoes, and beside it a low luggage rack at seat height. The light at the entrance is switched by a single, clearly visible switch which also activates the room's general lighting. The role these first metres play in every home is described in the article on the [entrance area](/magazin/eingangsbereich-ankommen-gestalten/).

Picture a guest opening the door for the first time. The hook is at hand height, the bench invites, the luggage rack waits at seat height, and one switch brings the room to life. Within a minute coat, shoes and bag have places, and the guest is already smiling.

What the guest sees on entering also matters. The first glance should fall on something beautiful: the window, a reading corner, a timber wall. Not on the bathroom door, not on the television. In an Altbau (the pre-1945 Viennese apartment building) this can often be solved simply by the position of the bed and a half-height built-in wall. But the first impression is only the beginning, because the night decides the rest.

## Sleep first: bed, blackout and acoustics

A guest forgives a great deal, but not a bad night. The bed is the most important piece of furniture in the room, and everything around the bed has to support sleep. That begins with the mattress and ends with the question of whether a standby light is glowing somewhere.

For good sleep in a hotel room, three things above all count:

- **Blackout:** Curtains or blinds that really make the room dark, including at the edges. In a Viennese Altbau with tall box-type windows, a curtain track integrated into the ceiling and extending beyond the window reveal is worthwhile.
- **Quiet:** Sound from the corridor, from the neighbouring room and from the street. Room doors with good seals, upholstered headboards on the party wall and heavy textiles help. Whether a party wall needs acoustic improvement, we clarify with a building physicist.
- **Control from the bed:** One switch for all the room's lighting and an individual reading light for each side, both reachable without getting up. Sockets and USB connections at bedside-table height.

For Clara and Martin the headboard became a full-height timber panel into which reading lights, switches and a slim shelf are recessed. The panelling also absorbs sound from the neighbouring room. How material, light and acoustics work together in a private bedroom is shown in the article [The bedroom as a place of rest](/magazin/schlafzimmer-ruhe-planen/); the principles apply in a hotel just the same.

Imagine two in the morning. A faint light near the floor shows the way, the room is dark and quiet, and the guest finds the bathroom without fumbling. And in the evening, which light?

## Light in scenes instead of one ceiling light

The central ceiling light is the quickest way to make a room feel impersonal. It flattens the room, creates harsh shadows and truly suits no time of day. A homely hotel room works with several light sources at different heights, combined into simple scenes.

| Scene | When | Light sources | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arriving | on entering | entrance light, indirect light on wall or curtain | bright enough to find your way, friendly |
| Living | in the evening | floor lamp at the reading corner, table lamp, indirect light | warm, about 2,700 kelvin, dimmed |
| Reading and sleeping | in bed | directed reading lights, rest off | pinpoint, glare-free for the second person |
| Night | at two o'clock | faint orientation light to the bathroom near the floor | barely perceptible, does not wake you |

For the guest, these scenes should be operable with a few clearly labelled or self-explanatory switches. A touch panel with twelve symbols is the opposite of home. The basics, from colour temperature to glare, are explained in the article [Lighting design for the home](/magazin/lichtplanung-wohnung-grundlagen/). Then comes the room every guest uses in private: the bathroom.

## The bathroom: homely, robust and quick to clean

In a hotel bathroom two demands meet that seem contradictory at first sight. The guest wants a bathroom that feels like a good private bathroom, with warm light, pleasant materials and enough shelf space. Housekeeping needs a bathroom that can be cleaned thoroughly in a few minutes, every day, over years.

Both can be reconciled if you economise in the right places and invest in the right places. Level-access showers with a linear drain instead of a point drain are comfortable for guests and simple to clean; how they are planned correctly is explained in the article on the [level-access shower](/magazin/bodengleiche-dusche-planen/). Large-format ceramic reduces the joints in which dirt collects. A wall-hung WC and a vanity unit with clear floor beneath make mopping easier.

The bathroom becomes homely through light and wood. A mirror with lateral, glare-free light makes faces look friendly, and a vanity base in oiled or lacquered wood brings warmth to an otherwise hard room. How the light at the mirror is planned is in the article on [bathroom lighting](/magazin/badbeleuchtung-spiegel-licht-planen/). For ceramics we choose, among other things, thin-walled washbasins, which look elegant even in small hotel bathrooms. And what must the materials in the room survive?

> A good hotel bathroom looks on the first day like a private bathroom and on the thousandth day still does.

## Materials that guests and housekeeping can withstand

A hotel room ages faster than a flat. Suitcases knock against edges, glasses leave rings, shoes stand on the carpet, and every day cleaning is done with professional products. The choice of materials decides whether a room has character after five years or looks worn.

Our experience from the joinery is clear: materials that age with dignity are the cheaper ones in the long run. Solid edges on tabletops instead of glued-on plastic edging. Oak or walnut with an oiled finish that can be repaired locally, instead of high gloss that shows every scratch. How these woods change over the years is described in the article on [real wood in interior fit-out](/magazin/echtholz-im-innenausbau-eiche-nuss/).

| Area | Proven in hotel operation | Delicate in hotel operation |
|---|---|---|
| Room floor | oiled parquet, robust carpet with wool content | soft softwood, pale deep-pile |
| Tabletops, shelves | veneer with solid edge, Dekton, ceramic | high-gloss lacquer, open-pored marble |
| Walls behind bed and luggage | timber panelling, upholstered panels, washable paint | delicate wallpaper, lime plaster without protection |
| Bathroom | large-format ceramic, Dekton, glass | micro-cement without consistent care |

For shelves and washbasins we like to use Cosentino Dekton, because the surface resists heat, scratches and stains while looking calm and high-quality. A detailed assessment of which surfaces truly last in daily use is offered by the article [Easy to care for? ## Furnishing a holiday apartment: interior design for longer stays

A holiday apartment is not a large hotel room. Guests often stay longer, cook for themselves, work in between and really unpack their suitcases. The interior design of a holiday apartment must therefore reflect more everyday life: a working kitchen, real storage, a place to work and a table at which to eat together.

Clara and Martin's two attic apartments are each around 45 square metres. We planned a single-run kitchen with full-value appliances, but without excess: two hobs are usually enough, a dishwasher is indispensable. How to plan a kitchen on little floor space that feels generous is shown in the article [Thinking big in a small kitchen](/magazin/kleine-kueche-gross-denken/). The dining table stands at the window and serves as a workplace during the day, with sockets near the table and a light that can do both.

For holiday apartments it also holds that everything the guest needs must be visible or easy to find, and everything only the operator needs must disappear. A lockable cupboard section for spare bed linen and cleaning products, a niche for the vacuum cleaner, a clearly marked place for instruction manuals. In Vienna, stricter rules have also applied since 2024 to the short-term letting of flats. Whether and to what extent a flat may be let to tourists should be clarified with the authority and the property management before planning; an overview of consents in an Altbau is given in the article [Conversions in a Viennese Altbau](/magazin/genehmigungen-altbau-wien-umbau/). ## Planning and execution during operation

The biggest challenge when fitting out a boutique hotel is often not the design but time. Every day a room cannot be let costs revenue. That is why we plan the execution so that rooms are renewed in sections and operations can continue.

The following sequence has proved its worth:

1. **Site survey and conversation:** We go through the house together, take measurements and talk about guests, target group, reception and housekeeping workflows and the budget.
2. **Concept for the whole house:** A material, lighting and colour concept that gives all rooms a shared character while still allowing variations, such as different wood tones per floor.
3. **Show room:** One room is fully completed and tested, by the operator, by housekeeping and ideally by first guests. Only then is the rest approved.
4. **Authorities and specialist planners:** Fire protection, escape routes, accessibility and building services are coordinated early with the responsible bodies and specialist planners.
5. **Prefabrication in the joinery:** Built-ins such as headboards, wardrobes, vanity bases and cabinets are made at Tischlerkultur while the rooms are still occupied.
6. **Installation in stages:** In each stage a few rooms are closed, installed and handed over cleaned, so that the rest of the house stays lettable.

Because planning and fabrication come from one coordinated hand, with one contact person for you and a joinery family-run since 1935, such time windows can be planned reliably. ## Frequently asked questions

### What distinguishes a boutique hotel room from a standard hotel room?

A standard hotel room is designed for repeatability, each room resembling the next. A boutique hotel room has a character of its own, arising from the house, the place and the attitude of the hosts. Functionally it must nevertheless be just as reliable: a good bed, blackout, quiet, light and an easy-to-clean bathroom. Personality on the surface, reliability underneath: that is the formula.

### Which materials suit hotel rooms that should feel homely?

Materials that age with dignity and can be repaired have proved their worth, such as oiled real wood, veneer with solid edges, robust textiles with wool content, large-format ceramic and ultra-compact surfaces like Dekton. Delicate are high-gloss surfaces, fragile wallpapers and open-pored natural stones without consistent care. The choice should always be made together with housekeeping, and we bring them into the planning from the start.

### How important is a show room?

A show room is highly recommended for hotels with several similar rooms. It shows whether light, workflows, cleaning and materials work in practice before a mistake is repeated in every room. Changes after the show room are comparatively cheap; changes after all rooms are finished are not. It is the cheapest insurance in the project.

### Can a hotel keep running during the redesign?

In many cases, yes, if the work is planned in stages. Built-ins are made in advance in the joinery, and only a few rooms are closed per stage. Noisy work such as chiselling or removing tiles is scheduled for times of day that disturb guests as little as possible. Your guests stay your guests.

### What does a holiday apartment need that a hotel room does not?

A holiday apartment needs a working kitchen with a dishwasher, real storage for unpacking, a dining table that doubles as a workplace, and lockable storage for the operator. Guests usually stay longer and live more of daily life in the apartment. The furnishing must therefore be more robust and more self-explanatory than in a private flat. We plan it together with your own workflows as operator.

### May any flat in Vienna be let as a holiday apartment?

No. Short-term tourist letting of flats has been more strictly regulated in Vienna since 2024 and is restricted in many cases. In addition, the tenancy agreement, the condominium agreement or the house rules may set requirements. Clarify these questions with the responsible authority and the property management before investing in the furnishing. In the first call we are happy to tell you what we would clarify first.
